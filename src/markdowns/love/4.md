This is a recall of when I first started meditation.

## A guide to 8 minutes meditation

It was an attempt to save myself from slipping into the bottom of emptiness. 27 years of age, I felt I was living everyday in pain. I constantly felt the engulf of flame each day because I was hyperactive most of the time. Before starting meditation, I was enrolled in a yoga program, and had been experiencing, on-and-off, the pre-yoga meditation sections. 

I was in love with that part of yoga, as well as yoga itself. I realized I occasionally craved the calm sitting in a quiet room. It was then when I encountered this book <b>8 minutes meditation</b>, which, needless to say, changed my life since then.

## Sign the contract

The book is so easy to read, with next to 0 academic level lexicon, while keeping the illustration filled with necessary complexity. One cute thing about this book is, after the beginner trial of an 8 minute mediation section, it will ask you if you want to keep doing meditation from then on, if so, you can sign the contract the book has provided in a certain page. I had vowed to never skip a day of meditation and it has not just ritualistically, but also realistically benefited me in the long run. 

## Meditative Vision 

Forgive me if I apply the wrong term for visionary during meditation, I called it "meditative vision" here for ease of use. It's one of the numerous meditation methods you can use during the practice. And this one is about an imaginary vision you have when you meditate. It could be a deity, a person, or a scenery. 

Care to guess what is my visionary? It's time to introduce my most beloved game series - <b>Witcher</b>. And my visionary is, of course, Geralt of Rivia.

I don't know why that game is so awesome, it makes my most beloved activity - meditation - as one of its core mechanic in the game. Mostly to generate health as well as alter the current day/ night cycle. But sometimes I still meditate and imagine Geralt with his meditative pose, sitting in a plain among grass nearby a swamp, and <b>the wind</b>, as always, <b>is howling</b>.

## Result

By now, I've meditated for years, and the peace and mindfulness it brings to my life is priceless and no amount of money is worth it. The only regret is I haven't done it as soon as I was conscious of who I am. I experience calmness that I wouldn've ever imagined possible. I lose control of myself less and life simply becomes more and more enjoyable each day. 

<b>Meditation is the cure to humanity.</b>
